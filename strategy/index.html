1. Overview & category

Current strategy — founder-approved revision

Lead with the whole shared home, not just the calendar. SplitHaus helps a group manage time, shared costs and practical house details. Scheduling remains the first interactive proof point and a natural setup entry—not the limit of the product promise.

The founder approved this broader direction after the council. The revised headline above is an editorial recommendation approved by the founder, not the council-selected headline. The category remains shared vacation-home coordination: no rental operations, banking or member-payment processing is implied. Show three concise capability links immediately below the hero, keep the scheduling demo first, retain substantive optional Finance visibility and add a house-guide example before the founder story.

Original council category decision (historical)

The council selects a single positioning category: shared vacation-home coordination. Rejected alternatives: shared calendar (understates allocation and shared-cost capability), property-management platform (implies rentals, vendors, banking or legal administration the product doesn't do), and equal scheduling-and-finance hero (adds a second evaluation task before scheduling is understood — kept only as a future research hypothesis).

Core promise & audience

Coordinate who goes when, keep shared costs clear, and put the house details where everyone can find them.

Audience: co-owners, friends and mixed groups sharing vacation homes, initially in Oregon and Washington. Primary conversion goal: a champion reaches a meaningful method-specific configuration review and can assess fit — account creation, membership, multi-member use and sustained adoption are tracked as separate outcomes, not assumed to follow.

Original council executive summary (historical, not the current headline direction)

Position SplitHaus as shared vacation-home coordination, lead with scheduling fit, and make optional shared-cost management a substantial second capability. Select "Plan who goes when." with anonymous setup as the primary action and an interactive sample calendar as the secondary action. Keep a forwardable homepage overview, create /pricing, and defer standalone /share and /how. Test corrected recipient proposals before building the integrated feature. The $400 figure increases finance research priority but does not establish processing demand, contribution margin or affordable CAC. No implementation, publication, analytics activation, outreach or spending is authorized.

2. Ranked changes & reasons

Nine ranked recommendations from the synthesis, with the founder-approved broader opening applied. Historical council preferences are preserved as provenance, not current instructions. Each states the current state, the proposed change and why it matters. Effort labels (S/M/L) are proposals, not commitments.

Rank 1 — Strategy & economics founder direction/hypothesis

Why it matters: the current homepage mixes fair-calendar and expense language; member-payment processing does not exist in the product. Without a clear category and an honestly-bounded economic model, the site risks implying a payments capability that isn't built and a revenue figure that isn't earned.

Change: adopt the shared vacation-home coordination category (above); make Finance a visible second capability with its own demo, terms and adoption measures; keep the $400/house/year figure as a founder hypothesis that raises finance research priority without implying it is proven. Effort: S. Proposed owner: Founders, Offer and Finance.

Rank 2 — Answers to founder's six questions

Why it matters: the founder needed decisions on journey, summary, pages, tone, disagreements and headline before design work could proceed.

  • Champion-first: yes, as the primary founder hypothesis; still preserve a homepage overview for groups that discuss before configuring.
  • Generic summary: shrink to a self-contained homepage section with a stable link; defer a standalone /share page.
  • How & pricing: create /pricing for complete base/add-on terms; defer /how until it would add real method detail beyond the homepage.
  • Tone: plain confident capability copy; keep audit caveats in working material, keep material customer limitations beside the relevant feature.
  • Material disagreements to keep visible: a tailored proposal can't always answer actual future dates; setup does not establish group agreement; named hypothetical outcomes require testing before use; generated individual subscription shares are omitted; Finance merits substantial visibility.
  • Headline: use "One shared home. A clearer way to manage it."; the subhead names time, costs and house details. Scheduling remains the first demonstration, without occupancy guarantees.

Rank 3 — Homepage copy (paste-ready)

Why it matters: current copy has a brand-only hero, universal-fit wording, unsupported setup-duration claims and an imprecise non-user answer.

Proposed copy

More than a shared calendar — A shared calendar shows bookings. SplitHaus also helps divide time through turns, draft picks or group preferences and shares. When plans change, your group can make unused dates available again.

What about our usual August week? — You can assign dates in a custom starting schedule and review them before saving. A rotation, draft or Match may produce a different arrangement. Check the weeks that matter to your group before choosing.

Will it work for how we share? (FAQ) — Explore taking turns, choosing dates in a draft, using Match or booking as you go. You can also assign a starting schedule yourself. Try the approach closest to yours and check the details that matter to your group.

Does everyone need an account? (FAQ) — People who join can book under their own names and receive their own reminders. You can also hold dates you're allowed to book for someone who doesn't use SplitHaus. Those dates appear under your name; that person won't receive their own reminders.

What does setup involve? (FAQ) — Add your groups, choose how you share time and review the schedule or scheduling rules. You can explore before creating an account. You'll need an account to save.

Five generations. One family house. — "I'm the fifth generation to share our family's house on the Oregon coast. We've tried phone calls, paper lists, texts, and shared calendars to figure out who goes when. None quite worked. I built SplitHaus to make that part easier." — Dan, founder

Keep shared costs clear, too. — Plan annual costs, split expenses, and track contributions, reserves and outside payments. Shared costs is an optional add-on: free for 90 days, then $79 per house, per year. Pay outside SplitHaus, then record and confirm the payment here. SplitHaus does not move money between members.

Demo caption: Sample calendar with fictional groups. Explore how each sharing method works. Billing terms: retain the published answers on no automatic trial-end charge, annual renewal, cancellation and separate billing per house; retain member viewing after lapse without strengthening it to say every role loses every write.

5. Economics

The $400 figure

founder direction/hypothesis The unit and period are settled by the founder's economics clarification and are not reopened by this report: $400 is a conservative gross annual revenue target, per house, per year, combining base SaaS revenue, Finance SaaS revenue and proposed transaction-processing revenue — before any costs. What remains genuinely unresolved is narrower than "the whole model": cohort and paid exposure, finance/processing adoption rates, eligible and routed transaction volume, fee-bearing legs, take rate, and processor/support/infrastructure costs. No CAC or payback floor is established from this figure alone.

Model

Gross revenue per house for a defined period equals realized base SaaS revenue plus realized Finance SaaS revenue plus adoption-weighted transaction-fee revenue.

Current published subscriptions: core scheduling is $119 per house, per year, after a free 1-year trial (no card to start). Finance (shared costs) is an optional add-on at $79 per house, per year, after a separate free 90-day trial. If both are paid for a full year, published SaaS revenue is $198 before costs — not zero, and not automatically $400.

The $202 gap, verified

Computed directly from the council's own illustration and checked with Python for this report:

Assumed take rate on one eligible fee-bearing legProcessing volume required to close the $202 gap
1.5%≈ $13,467
0.5%≈ $40,400
0.25%≈ $80,800

Verified: 202/0.015 = 13,466.67 → 13,467; 202/0.005 = 40,400.00; 202/0.0025 = 80,800.00 (computed with Python for this report).

Double-counting rule: track unique underlying funds separately from payment transactions. An incoming contribution and an outgoing bill can use the same dollars; count fee revenue on both legs only when policy and actual processing establish both charges.

Money movement today: no member money movement is implemented. The Finance copy states this explicitly — "Pay outside SplitHaus, then record and confirm the payment here. SplitHaus does not move money between members" — and this boundary must not be softened or reversed in any published copy until a processing feature actually ships and is separately authorized.

Acquisition implication: research groups with recurring scheduling and shared-cost needs, including a separate cost organizer where relevant; validate payer benefit and retained contribution before setting CAC or scaling any spend.

6. Founder decisions

Five decisions the founder must make before further design or build work proceeds. None of these are decided by this document.

  1. Headline & page order — headline approved: "One shared home. A clearer way to manage it."; three capability links, scheduling proof, fit, compact pricing, Finance, house-guide example, then founder story. Scope: craft choice; the alternative headline and claim-permission dissent remain recorded (Section 8).
  2. Minimum proposal scope — recommendation: reuse existing house saving and disclose its verified consequence; retain delayed-trial, proposal-only storage as a separately estimated option. Scope: product scope, not a silent change to trial policy.
  3. Proposal access & data policy — recommendation: choose expiry/code defaults, retention/deletion rules and permitted operational aggregation; exclude personalized proposals from replay. Scope: required before feature instrumentation and privacy promises.
  4. Current Hotjar/tracking treatment — recommendation: review actual auth/setup recording, masking, disclosure, consent and retention before any analytics activation or expansion. Scope: current-stack decision; vendor names alone don't establish adequacy.
  5. The $400 model & future payment customer benefit — recommendation: specify cohort, period, adoption, routed volume, fee payer/legs, costs and retention before acquisition economics are used for planning. Scope: economic research and future product planning only; no payment launch or spend.

7. Testing & measurement

Qualitative comprehension testing (proposed, not run)

3–5 consenting actual groups; each tests its relevant primary method (rotation/draft/Match). Recipients are first asked, unprompted, what they'd text the sender — then probed on actual-vs-illustrative dates, personal effort, missed draft turns, cost, trial timing, authority and money movement. Finance research includes the cost organizer when different, asking what existing bookkeeping solves and what would justify a future paid processing service. Interpretation is qualitative only; no significance, lift, or market-wide adoption claim follows.

PostHog measurement contract

Reused existing events: landing_view, landing_cta_clicked, pricing_viewed, pricing_objection_opened, demo/method events, wizard_started, step events, house_plan_review_viewed, wizard_save_started/failed, signup_started/completed, property_created, first_invite_sent, first_member_joined, first_reservation_made, member_returned_d7, and the Finance events finance_trial_started, finance_first_cost_posted, finance_first_settlement_recorded, finance_plan_published, finance_addon_activated, paid_conversion.

Proposed new events (for the not-yet-built recipient proposal): proposal_created, proposal_share_requested, proposal_share_result, proposal_viewed, proposal_join_opened, proposal_join_clicked.

Multi-member use is proposed as a server-derived milestone: a saved house plus verified non-organizer participation through a valid reservation, committed draft pick, or submitted Match preferences — explicitly not whole-group adoption.

Payload exclusions: names, emails, addresses, exact occupancy dates, ratings, notes, payment destinations, codes, tokens, raw private URLs; sanitize ctaHref plus automatic URL/referrer/error properties in both collectors. No proposal ID, token, or recipient-to-proposal linkage in ordinary persistent client analytics pending explicit policy.

Verification required before trusting any of this: trace authoritative outcomes to both collectors and aggregate reporting; test retries, pending replay, auth readiness, consent coverage and reset; define cohort windows and denominators (browser IDs are not people). This report does not activate analytics or authorize a replacement GA project.
